What Trace is

Trace is a client-side inspector that reports provenance signals found in a media file. It parses content credentials, metadata, watermark structure and forensic traces, and it summarises what it finds as a verdict. It runs entirely in your browser.

The software is licensed under the MIT licence. You may use, copy, modify and distribute it under those terms.

What the results mean

A result is a report of signals, not a statement of fact about the media. It is not a determination that an image or video is real, fake, human made or machine made. Absence of a signal is never evidence that content is authentic, and this tool does not describe any file as authentic, safe, verified real or not AI.

The heuristic checks, including the watermark structure probe, error level analysis, the spectral grid search, the noise residual estimate and the visible mark scan, can be wrong in both directions. False positives are common on screenshots, re-encoded images and genuine photographs. Do not rely on this tool as the sole basis for a decision about authenticity, authorship, legality or honesty.

Signatures and authority

Trace reads a signed C2PA manifest when one is present, but it does not verify the cryptographic signature in the browser. A parsed manifest is a claim by whoever signed it, not a proof. The only vendor-authoritative result Trace can display is a live response from Google's SynthID API, and that check runs only when you configure a key that has SynthID access.
